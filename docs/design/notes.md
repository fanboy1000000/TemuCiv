**2026-09-30: Buildings that age, and renovation**
In Civ 7 I like the idea that buildings degrade over time and have to be replaced. I don't like the sudden switch
of them being outdated. Instead we should have a feature that lets you renovate them. However, we need to think
about how that works together with unlocking new buildings through science.

**2026-09-30: Civ switching, equalizers and the endgame**
In Civ 7 I don't like the age transitions. But I like choosing new civs and making new combos based on where you
are in the game. I also like that it is an equalizer. A problem civ games have is that a good start gets you way
too far ahead of everybody. The snowball is too significant for a long game, as the endgame becomes boring. A fun
endgame is a really hard part of the game design.

**2026-09-30: The snowball, continued: hardship makes stronger armies**
Regarding the snowball problem: I like the idea that civs that are behind can create stronger military units.
Hard times create hard men, soft times create soft men.

**2026-09-30: Multiple paths to victory**
I like the idea that there are multiple paths to victory: military, expansionist, scientific, cultural, etc.

**2026-09-30: Build your leader like a Path of Exile skill tree**
I love the skill tree from Path of Exile, which gives you endless possibilities. Instead of choosing a leader, you
create your leader through the choices you make.

**2026-10-19: Districts as a mix of buildings**
Building a district on a tile should not just be building one building. Instead, you add different buildings to
that area of the city. The optimal mix should depend on things like sanitation, population density, the natural
yields on the tile (wood, stone, etc.), combinations of buildings and so on. These are still loose ideas.
*(written down by the Orchestrator at the owner's request; → parking lot, S5; the paused brainstorm is in the Sprint 5
log, "Districts brainstorm")*


**2026-10-23: Production, population, tax and stability**
Production determiines how fast things are produced, as decided earlier. Food gives population. Population is used
for armies, and for tax base. You can set your tax rate, and the higher it is, the lower stability. However, a higher
tax lets you invest in things giving stability. A higher popuation gives you a higher tax base. Trade will also still
generate money..
